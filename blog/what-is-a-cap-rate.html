What Is a Cap Rate? Net Operating Income Divided by Price

A cap rate is one division problem. Here is the math in two lines, and what the number can and cannot tell you.

So, what is a cap rate? It is a property's net operating income divided by its price. Net operating income is the income left after operating expenses, before any mortgage payment. Because it ignores financing, a cap rate lets you compare buildings of different sizes on the same footing.

The formula

Cap rate = net operating income ÷ price.

Operating expenses come out of the income first. The mortgage payment does not, which is why the cap rate is a view of the building itself, before any loan.

An arithmetic illustration

Arithmetic illustration only (not a real listing, price or projected return): $50,000 of net operating income on an $800,000 price is 6.25%. Raise the price to $1,000,000 with the same income and it drops to 5%.

Same income, higher price, lower cap rate.

Three questions before you trust any cap rate

  • Is the income actual, or a projection of what the building "could" earn?
  • Which expenses are in it (taxes, insurance, repairs, management, vacancy)?
  • Are you comparing similar buildings in similar areas?

The number is only as good as the inputs. Two buildings can show the same cap rate and mean very different things if one counts every expense and the other leaves some out.

How we use it

Our Top Investments lists use only listings that report both expenses and net operating income. Those figures are broker-reported, not independently verified. For how we apply the method to a ranked list, see the Higher-Income Multifamily Shortlist.

The SavingsRE take
  • Cap rate is net operating income divided by price, before any mortgage payment.
  • Check whether the income is actual or projected, and which expenses were counted.
  • Broker-reported figures are a starting point to verify, not a conclusion.
