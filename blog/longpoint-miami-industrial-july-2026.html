Longpoint Pays $273 a Foot for Miami Infill Industrial

The week's top commercial trade wasn't the biggest. It was the most expensive per square foot — and that's the number that matters.

A two-building warehouse complex at 8452 Northwest 72nd Street in Miami, spanning nearly 90,000 square feet, sold for $24.6 million — roughly $273 per square foot. A trust sold it to an affiliate of Boston-based Longpoint. It was the most expensive commercial transaction to hit records that week.

Put it next to the other industrial headline of this cycle. The Sun Sentinel's ~382,000-square-foot printing complex in Deerfield Beach traded for $65 million — a far bigger check, but on those reported figures it works out to roughly $170 a foot. Miami-Dade infill is clearing at something like a 60% premium per square foot to Broward big-box. Same asset class, different product: one is scarce, small-bay, close-in space near the airport with no realistic path to new supply; the other is large-format space where land still exists.

That spread is the whole thesis. Institutional capital isn't buying "industrial" — it's buying irreplaceable location and the pricing power that comes with it. For a local investor, the read-through is that the Miami-Dade infill bid is now institutional, which means you're competing with cost-of-capital that is lower than yours. Meanwhile our multifamily comps show Miami-Dade around 5.79%, Fort Lauderdale/Broward 6.27% and Palm Beach/Boca 4.98% — the yield you can still find is in Broward, not in the Miami infill lane where the institutions have parked.

The SavingsRE take
  • Never underwrite Miami-Dade infill off Broward big-box comps. The per-foot spread this week was roughly $273 vs. $170 — that's a different market, not a different building.
  • If you're bidding infill Miami-Dade industrial against institutions, you win on speed, certainty and off-market access — not on price.
  • Broward is where a private investor's yield still lives. Let the institutions have the trophy infill.
