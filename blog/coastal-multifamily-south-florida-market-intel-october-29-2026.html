Coastal Multifamily Near the Ocean: South Florida Market Intel, October 29, 2026

Coastal multifamily between Hollywood and Boynton Beach is listing at roughly 7% to 9.4% cap this week. Here is that list, September's county numbers, and where rates and insurance stand for investors buying near the water.

Coastal multifamily: this week's list

Our Top Investments screen for coastal multifamily along the Atlantic beaches covers income properties from Hollywood and Fort Lauderdale up to Boynton Beach, priced $1M to $5M and ranked by cap rate. This week's top six run from about 7.2% to about 9.4% cap, and three of them are price reduced:

The rest of the top 10 runs from about 7.0% to 7.2% cap, including a 9-unit in a Hollywood Opportunity Zone reported fully occupied and a 13-unit value-add property in Fort Lauderdale. Several of the leaders have been on the market 90 to 180 days or more, which can leave room to negotiate.

For context, institutional multifamily cap rates run about 6.27% in Fort Lauderdale/Broward, 5.79% in Miami-Dade and 4.98% in Palm Beach/Boca Raton. Fort Lauderdale ranked as the top U.S. market for multifamily investment profitability through late 2025, Broward multifamily sales volume reached $1.28B (up about 20% year over year), and net absorption is outpacing new deliveries in Broward and Palm Beach. A cap well above those benchmarks usually means a seller-stated figure, a value-add property or below-market rents, so treat the list as a place to start underwriting.

Rates and insurance

The daily lender average for a 30-year fixed was 7.33% on October 26, and the 30-year jumbo averaged 7.60%. Freddie Mac's weekly survey had the 30-year at 7.32% for the week of October 22, down from 7.40%. For most South Florida counties the 2026 jumbo threshold is $832,750.

Insurance is part of every coastal hold-cost calculation. Approved homeowners rate filings in the southeast Florida counties took effect July 1 at -11% to -14%, four more homeowners rate cuts were approved in September (average -7%), and Citizens' policy count is down about 81% from its peak. Coastal, older and condo properties may see less relief than inland single-family homes.

The SavingsRE take
  • Investors: the coastal list's top caps sit two to three points above the institutional benchmarks. Ask for the rent roll and trailing expenses, and price insurance before you write an offer.
  • Sellers: inventory is down about a fifth from a year ago, so there is less competition for your listing.
  • Buyers: if you are above $832,750, price your payment off the jumbo rate.
