1031 Exchange 45 and 180 Day Rules: Two Clocks That Start Together

Understanding the 1031 exchange 45 and 180 day rules is the first step for any investor who is selling property and planning to buy again.

The 1031 exchange 45 and 180 day rules are the two numbers every like-kind exchange runs on. One is the window for identifying what you will buy next; the other is the deadline for actually receiving it. This note walks through both, using the IRS instructions for Form 8824 as the source.

The 45-day identification window

Per the IRS instructions for Form 8824, replacement property must be identified within 45 days after the property you are giving up is transferred. Identification is the first of the two clocks, and it is the shorter one.

The 180-day closing window

The replacement property must be received within 180 days of the transfer or by your tax return due date (including extensions), whichever is earlier. If your return due date falls before the 180 days run out, the earlier date controls.

Both clocks start together

The two clocks run at the same time. The 180 days are not counted after the 45 days end. If you are selling, it can help to start looking at replacement properties early, so the 45 days are spent choosing, not searching.

The SavingsRE take
  • Know your buy box before you sell: property type, area and the kind of income you want.
  • Start the replacement search early, so the identification window is used for choosing.
  • Bring your tax adviser and qualified intermediary in at the start.

What qualifies

Since 2018, like-kind exchanges apply only to real property held for business or investment use.

General information, not tax advice

This is general information only, not tax advice. Your tax adviser and qualified intermediary decide the details of your exchange, including how the dates apply to your situation.
