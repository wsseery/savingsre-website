Florida Seller Flood Disclosure: Three Questions Before the Contract Is Signed

The form comes with the contract, not with the closing paperwork. Here is what it asks and what it defines.

The Florida seller flood disclosure is easy to overlook because it is one form among many. Under Fla. Stat. 689.302, a seller of residential real property must complete a flood disclosure and give it to the buyer at or before the time the sales contract is signed. Not at closing. Before the contract, or with it.

When the disclosure is due

The timing is the part that catches people. A seller who waits until the closing table has waited too long. The statute ties the disclosure to the moment the contract is executed, so a seller who plans to list should have the answers ready before an offer arrives. A buyer should expect to see the form as part of the contract package and should read it before signing.

The three questions

The current form, as amended effective October 1, 2025, asks the seller three things:

  1. Do you know of any flooding that damaged the property during your ownership?
  2. Have you filed a claim with an insurance provider for flood damage to the property, including with the National Flood Insurance Program?
  3. Have you received assistance for flood damage to the property, including from FEMA?

The first question is limited to the seller’s time of ownership. It asks about flooding the seller knows of that damaged the property while the seller owned it. The second and third questions are about the paper trail: an insurance claim, including one with the National Flood Insurance Program, and any assistance received, including from FEMA.

What the statute means by “flooding”

The statute defines flooding, and the definition is broader than a storm surge. It includes the overflow of inland or tidal waters, rapid runoff from an established water source such as a river or drainage ditch, and sustained standing water from rainfall. When the form asks about flooding, those are the events it has in mind.

Where the form comes from

The disclosure requirement is in Fla. Stat. 689.302, and the current wording reflects amendments in Laws of Florida ch. 2024-215 and ch. 2025-166. If you want the exact text, read the statute itself rather than a summary, including this one.

For sellers: gather your records before you list

Because the form is due at or before the contract, the time to prepare is before the listing goes live. Look back through your ownership for any flooding that damaged the property. Find any insurance claims you filed, including any with the National Flood Insurance Program. Find any assistance you received, including from FEMA. Accurate answers start with accurate records.

For buyers: read it before you sign

Treat the completed form as a starting point for your own questions. It reports what the seller knows and has done during the seller’s ownership. It is not a substitute for your own review of the property and the documents, and it is a fair prompt to ask your agent and your other advisors what the answers mean for you.

The SavingsRE take
  • The flood disclosure is due at or before the time the sales contract is signed, not at closing.
  • Since October 1, 2025, the form asks three questions: flooding that damaged the property, a flood-damage insurance claim, and assistance received, including from FEMA.
  • Sellers should pull their records together before listing. Buyers should read the form before signing.

Source: Fla. Stat. 689.302; Laws of Florida ch. 2024-215 and ch. 2025-166, read Oct 20, 2026.
