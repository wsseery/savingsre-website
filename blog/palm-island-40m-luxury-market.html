$40M on Palm Island: The Trophy Market Keeps Setting the Pace

A double-lot Miami Beach estate sold for $40 million to a venture investor and a hedge-fund co-founder. At the very top, South Florida still isn’t slowing down.

The week’s top recorded residential sale was a double-lot estate at 145–149 Palm Avenue on Miami Beach’s Palm Island, which homebuilder Adrian Pedro sold for $40 million to venture investor Ben Ling and Hedge Labs co-founder Christopher Coudron. Buyers like these — tech and finance principals relocating capital and residence to Florida — are the engine that has kept the trophy tier resilient while the broader market normalized.

The important detail is the double lot. At this level, buyers are increasingly paying for irreplaceable land and assemblage potential, not just the house on it. Guard-gated island dirt with deep-water frontage is a fixed supply against a still-growing pool of ultra-high-net-worth demand, which is why these prices hold even as more ordinary segments negotiate.

For luxury sellers, the lesson is that provenance, land, and privacy still command full pricing power — but only when the story is clean and the comps are current. For buyers, the trophy market rewards conviction and speed; the best island and oceanfront assets rarely sit.

The SavingsRE take
  • Ultra-luxury demand is capital-migration driven — structurally sticky, not rate-sensitive.
  • At the top, buyers pay for land and scarcity; assemblage lots trade at a premium.
  • Luxury sellers: lead with land, privacy, and provenance — and price to live comps.
