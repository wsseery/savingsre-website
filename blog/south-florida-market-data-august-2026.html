South Florida Market Data — Prices Hold, Mortgage Rates Tick Up to 6.66%

June county medians are still climbing, but the cost of money moved the other way this week: the 30-year is back up to 6.66%. Here's the by-the-numbers read for SE-Florida buyers and investors.

The headline this week is financing, not prices. The 30-year fixed ticked up to 6.66% — from 6.58% a week ago, and now above the 6.72% of a year ago only by a hair. Jumbo money, which prices a large share of our coastal and investment purchases, is near 6.85%. It's a small move, but the direction matters: buyers who were waiting for rates to keep falling just got a reminder that the trend isn't a straight line down. If you're financing, price the deal at today's rate and lock — don't underwrite to a cut that keeps slipping.

Demand hasn't blinked. Closed sales rose for a tenth straight month, up 18.6% year over year in June, and year-to-date sales are up 7.9% against just 2.2% nationally. Palm Beach still leads the county table with double-digit appreciation while the rest of the region posts low single digits — so the "South Florida market" is really six different markets, and the number that matters depends on which county your thesis sits in. On the commercial side, Fort Lauderdale was ranked the No. 1 U.S. market for multifamily investment profitability, and Broward multifamily sales volume hit $1.28 billion, up 20% year over year.

Yields are where the data gets actionable. This week's refreshed Top Investment lists put NNN net-lease deals at cap rates to roughly 9.3%, now tagged with lease-term (WALT) and Opportunity-Zone context, alongside a fresh set of Atlantic-beach multifamily income deals across the Fort Lauderdale corridor. And the quiet tailwind under all of it is insurance: Florida coastal rate filings are down 11–14%, so the carry on a coastal asset is cheaper than it was a year ago — which does more for real returns than a few basis points on the note.

The SavingsRE take
  • The 6.58% → 6.66% uptick is a nudge, not a shock — but it ends the "wait for lower rates" logic. Financed buyers: re-run DSCR at 6.66–6.85% before you write.
  • County medians held flat month-over-month; the appreciation story is still Palm Beach (+11.8%), not the whole region.
  • Cap rates to ~9.3% on NNN are a screening starting point — verify WALT, tenant credit, and the T-12, not the flyer.
  • Cheaper insurance (filings −11–14%) is the underrated variable — it lifts net yield on coastal assets more than the rate move costs.
