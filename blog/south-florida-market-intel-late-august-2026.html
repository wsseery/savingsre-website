South Florida Market Data — Mortgage Rates Slip to 6.65%, Multifamily Yields Cross 10%

The cost of money moved the buyer's way this week — the 30-year fell for a second straight week — while county medians held and the highest-yield multifamily deals crossed a 10% cap. Here's the late-August read for SE-Florida investors.

The headline this week is financing. The 30-year fixed slipped to 6.65% — down from 6.67% a week ago and its second straight weekly decline, versus 6.58% a year ago (Freddie Mac). The 15-year fell to 5.95%, and jumbo money — which prices a large share of our coastal and investment purchases — eased to 6.78% from 6.84%. It's a modest move, but the direction finally favors buyers who've been on the sidelines. If you're financing, this is the week to get pre-approved and lock; a two-week trend is worth acting on, not waiting out.

At the top of the market, velocity is accelerating. Million-dollar-plus sales rose 22% in Q1 across SE Florida year over year, Miami-Dade's $1M+ unit sales were up 14.7% in May, and 2025 logged 361 sales over $10M — the most since 2021. Palm Beach County's average price is running $1.025M year to date, up 11%. Condos are the split-screen: single-family medians held and rose, but tri-county condo sales volume cooled sharply — down more than 20% year over year — as buyers price in insurance and assessment risk. On the commercial side, Fort Lauderdale was ranked the No. 1 U.S. market for multifamily investment profitability, and Broward multifamily sales volume hit $1.28 billion, up 20% year over year.

The yield story is a barbell. In the core coastal metros, multifamily cap rates are compressed — roughly 6.27% in Fort Lauderdale/Broward, 5.79% in Miami-Dade, and just 4.98% in Palm Beach/Boca — with asking rents near $2,400–$2,550 and vacancy in the 7–9% range. Investors chasing double-digit going-in yields are finding them a step outside the core, in the Sarasota, Daytona and Vero Beach corridors, where 8-to-13-unit deals are penciling 9.4–10.3%. The quiet tailwind under all of it is insurance: coastal filings have eased, though older homes, condos and true coastal exposure see less relief than inland single-family — so underwrite the premium, don't assume the average.

The SavingsRE take
  • Two straight weekly declines to 6.65% ends the "keep waiting" logic — get pre-approved and lock now; re-run DSCR at 6.65–6.78% before you write.
  • Single-family medians held and Palm Beach still leads on appreciation (+7.6%) — but condo sales volume is down 20%+, so condo pricing power and financing are county- and building-specific.
  • Core coastal multifamily caps are sub-6% to ~6.3%; the 9.4–10.3% deals are one market out — verify WALT, the T-12, and reserves, not the flyer.
  • Cheaper coastal insurance lifts net yield more than the rate move — but coastal, older, and condo risk gets less of the discount. Get a real quote before you model it.
