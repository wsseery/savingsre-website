Mortgage Rates Climb to 6.55% — A Second Straight Weekly Rise

Two consecutive increases, and the 15-year moved further than the 30-year. The "wait for the cut" trade is getting expensive.

Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed at 6.55% for the week of July 16, up from 6.49% — a second straight weekly increase. The 15-year moved further, jumping 11 basis points to 5.93% from 5.82%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.75%, so financing is still cheaper than last summer, but the direction has flipped. Bankrate's 30-year jumbo average was 6.63% as of July 15. Freddie Mac's own read: purchase-application demand has weakened recently, while affordability and inventory are improving for prospective buyers.

That last point deserves a South Florida asterisk. Nationally, rising inventory is what's cushioning affordability. Down here it isn't happening. Our county data still shows supply contracting year over year — homes for sale are down 21.3% in South Palm Beach County, 16.5% in Broward, 14.8% in North Palm Beach and 15.2% on the Treasure Coast. SE Florida buyers are absorbing the rate increase without the inventory relief the national narrative assumes. That combination favors sellers of well-priced product and punishes buyers waiting for rates and supply to break their way at the same time.

For financed investors the move is small but not free. Six basis points on the conforming rate is noise. The 11-basis-point jump on the 15-year is not, if short-amortization debt is how you were penciling the deal. Re-run debt-service coverage before you write, not after the lender does it for you. For cash and jumbo buyers — still a large share of coastal and trophy activity here — this week changes essentially nothing about what a specific address is worth.

The SavingsRE take
  • Two straight increases is a direction, not a blip. Stop underwriting to a rate cut that keeps not arriving.
  • The national "inventory is rising" story does not describe SE Florida, where supply is still down double digits YoY. Don't import that assumption into your offer.
  • Financed buyers: re-run DSCR at 6.5–6.75%. Cash and jumbo buyers: keep negotiating on price and terms, not headlines.
