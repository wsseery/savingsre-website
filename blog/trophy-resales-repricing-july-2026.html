Star Island at $55M, Venetian at $42.5M — Trophy Resales Are Repricing

Four eight-figure resales in a single week. The interesting number isn't the price — it's the hold period.

Sean Combs sold the 8,000-square-foot estate at 1 Star Island Drive in Miami Beach for $55 million to JFStar LLC, led by John A. Franklin of Virginia Beach. He had paid $35 million for it in 2021. Two days earlier, a 2025-built waterfront home at 416 West San Marino Drive on the Venetian Islands traded for $42.5 million; it last sold in 2024 for $23.1 million, with Long Light Capital CEO Justin Korsant as the seller. In Palm Beach, a trust tied to the late Prince Albrecht of Oettingen-Spielberg parted with the roughly 5,900-square-foot oceanfront home at 143 East Inlet Drive for $31.8 million after decades of family ownership. And in Coral Gables, 221 Casuarina Concourse fetched $24.5 million while 9360 Balada Street traded at $20.5 million.

Read the hold periods, not the headlines. Star Island returned roughly 57% over about five years. The Venetian house nearly doubled — $23.1M to $42.5M — in about two, but that isn't market drift: the home was rebuilt in 2025, so the buyer paid for new construction on a finished waterfront lot, not for two years of appreciation. Conflating those two is how investors talk themselves into bad comps.

The ask-versus-trade spread is the sharper signal. The Palm Beach oceanfront home printed above its $30.3 million last ask. The Coral Gables mansion on Casuarina printed at $24.5 million against a $28 million ask — roughly 12% under. Same week, same luxury tier, opposite outcomes. Pricing power in this market is not a "South Florida" attribute; it's an address attribute. Barrier island and direct oceanfront still clears at or above ask because the supply is finite. Large inland-adjacent estates negotiate, because they compete with each other.

The SavingsRE take
  • Don't underwrite a Venetian-style "double" as market appreciation — that was a rebuild premium. Separate the land, the build, and the market in every comp.
  • Direct oceanfront and barrier-island trophy product is still a seller's negotiation; Gables-tier estates are a buyer's. Price the street, not the county.
  • If you're holding trophy product bought in 2021 or earlier, this week priced your exit. That window is worth testing while it's open.
