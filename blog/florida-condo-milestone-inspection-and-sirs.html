Florida Condo Milestone Inspection and SIRS: Two Building Reports to Ask For

Two reports that tell you about the building, not just the unit, and what Florida law says you can ask for.

If you are shopping for a condo, the Florida condo milestone inspection and SIRS are two building reports that belong on your reading list. One is a structural inspection. The other is a reserve study. Both look at the building as a whole, which is what you share ownership of when you buy a unit. For a wider look at what to compare, browse our condo listings and keep these two reports in mind as you compare buildings.

Why look at the building, not only the unit

A condo purchase is two decisions in one: the unit you will live in and the building around it. Finishes and views are easy to see on a tour. The roof, the structure, the plumbing and the windows are harder to judge by eye, and they are shared. That is why these two reports matter. They are written about the building, they come from the rules Florida sets for condo associations, and they give you something concrete to read before you commit.

The milestone inspection (Fla. Stat. 553.899)

For condo and co-op buildings three habitable stories or more, a structural inspection by a licensed architect or engineer is due by December 31 of the year the building reaches 30 years from its certificate of occupancy. It repeats every 10 years after that.

The local building agency may require it at 25 years where local conditions, such as proximity to salt water, call for it.

In plain terms, this is a check on the building’s structure by a licensed professional, repeated on a schedule. If you are looking at an older building, ask whether the inspection applies and where it stands.

The structural integrity reserve study, or SIRS (Fla. Stat. 718.112)

Residential condo associations must have a SIRS at least every 10 years for each building three habitable stories or higher. It looks at items such as the roof, structure, fireproofing, plumbing, electrical, waterproofing and windows, and it recommends a reserve funding plan.

Think of the SIRS as the association’s plan for the big-ticket building items. It names the components it reviewed and recommends how the association should fund reserves for them. If you want to understand how an association is preparing for future work, this is the document that speaks to it.

What buyers can ask for (Fla. Stat. 718.503)

Once you are under contract on a resale unit, Florida law generally entitles you, at the seller’s expense, to two things: the summary of the milestone inspection report (if applicable) and the association’s most recent SIRS, or a statement that none has been completed.

Read them alongside the association budget. The inspection summary speaks to the condition of the structure. The SIRS speaks to how the association plans to fund the work. Together with the budget, they give you a fuller picture of the building than the unit itself can.

A short checklist for your next tour

  • Ask whether the building is three habitable stories or more, since that is where both reports apply.
  • Ask when the milestone inspection is due, or whether it has been completed, if it applies.
  • Ask for the most recent SIRS, or a statement that none has been completed.
  • Read both alongside the association budget, and bring questions to your agent and, where appropriate, your attorney.
The SavingsRE take
  • The milestone inspection is a structural inspection by a licensed architect or engineer for buildings three habitable stories or more.
  • The SIRS looks at building components and recommends a reserve funding plan.
  • Once under contract on a resale unit, ask for the inspection summary (if applicable) and the most recent SIRS, or the statement that none has been completed.
