Florida Separate Roof Deductible: The Page Behind the Declarations Page

If you are reviewing a seller’s insurance paperwork, or your own, here is a page worth turning to.

A Florida separate roof deductible is a feature some homeowners policies carry, and Florida law says exactly where it has to be disclosed. If a policy has one, Fla. Stat. §627.701 requires the disclosure on the page immediately behind the declarations page, with no other policy language on that page, in boldfaced type no smaller than 18 point.

What the statute dictates

The statute does not leave the wording to the insurer. The page must state that the policyholder is electing coverage containing a separate deductible for roof losses, that this may result in high out-of-pocket expenses, and that they should discuss it with their insurance agent.

When a legislature specifies the placement and the minimum point size, it is telling you how often this surprised people.

A trade, not a trap

A separate roof deductible is not a trap. It is a trade: a different deductible structure applied to roof losses specifically, distinct from the rest of the policy and from the hurricane deductible. Some owners take that trade knowingly. The statute’s concern is the word knowingly.

The same subsection requires the actual dollar value of the roof deductible to appear on the declarations page or the premium renewal notice, at issuance and at renewal, so the number is findable.

What it means in a transaction

Buyers and sellers both end up looking at insurance paperwork. When you ask for the declarations page, turn one page further. If a separate roof deductible is there, you will see it in plain, boldfaced language, and you can find the dollar value on the declarations page or the renewal notice. Then you can decide what it means for you, and talk it through with your own insurance agent.

The SavingsRE take
  • Turn one page past the declarations page.
  • A separate roof deductible is a legitimate trade that some owners choose knowingly.
  • The dollar value must appear on the declarations page or the renewal notice.
