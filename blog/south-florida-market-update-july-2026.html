South Florida Mid-Summer Update — Prices Up, Insurance Easing, Cap Rates to 9.4%

Medians still climbing, financing holding, coastal insurance finally softening, and Atlantic-beach multifamily trading at yields that keep pulling capital in. The mid-summer read in one screen.

Palm Beach is the standout — double-digit gains while the rest of the region posts low singles — and volume backs it: closed sales rose a tenth straight month (+18.6% YoY in June), YTD sales +7.9% vs. 2.2% nationally, inventory still down ~22–24%. A demand story, not a fire sale.

Financing is steady, not falling: 30-year fixed 6.58% (vs. 6.74% a year ago), jumbo ~6.78%. The bigger shift is insurance — SE-Florida filings approved 11–14% lower (effective July 1), and Citizens down to ~336K policies (−76% from its 2023 peak) as private carriers return. Lower carry on the two line items that scared buyers off is what widens who can transact. If you haven't re-shopped a coastal policy since the cuts landed, it's worth a fresh look — get an insurance quote ↗.

Commercial is where the yield sits. Fort Lauderdale ranked the No. 1 U.S. multifamily market for profitability; Broward MF volume hit $1.28B (+20% YoY). The deals: ~10 Atlantic-beach multifamily assets across Fort Lauderdale, Pompano, and Deerfield ($1–5M) at broker-reported caps of 7.1–9.4%, with statewide 8%+ leaders quoting 12–14%. At the top, Q1 logged 3,382 sales above $1M (+22% YoY), the luxury threshold reset to $3.3M from $2.7M, and a West Palm non-waterfront trade set a record at $15.7M (~$1,820/SF). Meanwhile commercial construction cooled — sales −13% to $5.42B in H1 — tightening future supply for anyone buying existing cash flow now.

The SavingsRE take
  • Palm Beach's 11.8% jump isn't the whole region — pick the county to your thesis; appreciation and cap rate live in different submarkets.
  • The real 2026 unlock isn't rates, it's insurance: 11–14% lower premiums change the underwriting math on coastal multifamily more than a quarter-point on the note.
  • Broker-reported caps of 7.1–9.4% on Atlantic-beach deals are a screening starting point, not verified yield — trust the T-12, not the flyer.
  • Cooling construction plus down inventory means existing cash-flowing assets get scarcer, not cheaper. Buy the operations, not the forecast.
