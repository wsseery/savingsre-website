About This Search

This search tracks triple-net (NNN) leased investment property priced from $1 million to $10 million across Palm Beach, Broward and Miami-Dade: retail, office, industrial and mixed-use buildings and units whose listings describe a net lease.

The cap rate on a NNN property depends on the lease: the tenant, the years remaining, the rent increases and who covers the roof and structure. Most sellers do not publish their income in the MLS, so Bill requests the lease and rent roll for any listing you want to compare.

The search is refreshed weekly with the current count and a link to every active listing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does triple-net (NNN) mean?

The tenant pays the property's real estate taxes, insurance and maintenance on top of base rent, so the owner's income is closer to the rent itself. Some leases leave roof or structure with the landlord, so read each lease.

Why don't most listings show a cap rate?

Many NNN sellers keep income out of the MLS and share it after a buyer signs a confidentiality agreement. Bill requests the lease, rent roll and offering memorandum for the listings you pick.

Are NNN properties good for a 1031 exchange?

They are a common choice because they are easy to identify and manage. Exchange rules and deadlines are strict, so use a qualified intermediary and your tax adviser.
